Player guide

How We Play

The Quiet Ones is a shared map game about a community living through one uneasy year. You do not need a finished character sheet. You need attention, restraint, and a willingness to let the map remember things before anyone explains them.

Your role

You speak for currents in the community

Not one main character

You may name individual people, but you are not playing one permanent protagonist. On your turn you might speak for a family, a faction, a rumor, an elder, a child, a work crew, or the whole settlement's mood.

Care, then complicate

Care about whether the community survives. Also introduce problems, mistakes, strange opportunities, and hard choices. The best turns make success feel possible without making it simple.

Principles

What good play looks like

01

Draw small, clear marks. The map can be symbolic, literal, ugly, beautiful, incomplete, or all of those at once.

02

Ask what the community would notice. The answer does not need to reveal the whole truth.

03

Let disagreement exist. You can record tension without solving it immediately.

04

When you add something strange, ground it in a practical consequence: a route changes, a job becomes dangerous, a tradition shifts, a resource becomes harder to trust.

05

Leave some doors closed. The Quiet Ones works best when the table preserves mysteries for later Stellar Dawn play.

Tone

How this version differs

Local horror

The danger should feel close: wells, roads, schools, work songs, weather, ledgers, old customs, and rooms people avoid.

Soft apocalypse

The world is wounded, but people still cook, repair, argue, flirt, bargain, pray, and make plans for next week.

Unreliable records

Contradictions are useful. A false map, missing name, or changed memory can matter more than a monster reveal.

Future canon

What you create may become ruins, rumors, factions, relics, or unfinished business in Stellar Dawn.
