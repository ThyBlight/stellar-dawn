Player's handbook

Equipment Flavor

Choose normal fifth edition equipment for rules purposes. The table may rename, redescribe, or visually reinterpret that equipment to fit Stellar Dawn, but the item keeps its normal mechanics unless a rule explicitly changes them.

Core rule

Flavor Does Not Change Crunch

Reskinning an item changes what it looks like in the fiction. It does not change cost, weight, armor class, damage, range, properties, requirements, ammunition, or limitations unless the DM approves a specific rule change.

Armor

Armor Keeps Its Rules

Light armor might be an armored flight suit, medium armor might be a tactical vacsuit, and heavy armor might be reinforced combat plate or powered-looking exo-plate. If the original armor does not require a power source, the Stellar Dawn version does not require one for rules purposes.

Shields

Shields Stay Shields

A shield might be a hard-light buckler, grav-deflector, riot slab, collapsible boarding shield, or defensive drone plate. It still uses normal shield rules unless a specific item says otherwise.

Gear

Ordinary Gear Can Look Modern

Adventuring gear may be redescribed as survival equipment, station tools, field kits, sealed containers, utility bands, scanners, or shipboard supplies while keeping its listed rules and cost.

Weapons

Melee Still Matters

Stellar Dawn keeps fifth edition's weapon rules. The setting explains why close combat remains practical without requiring new weapon mechanics.

Setting Logic

Why Blades and Batons Survive

Melee weapons remain common because shipboard combat, station law, armor technology, shield systems, close quarters, ammunition concerns, and collateral damage make blades, batons, polearms, and impact weapons practical.

Examples

Reskin by Role

A longsword might be a mono-blade or boarding saber. A quarterstaff might be a shock baton. A warhammer might be a grav-hammer. A longbow might be a coilbow, flechette caster, or other silent launcher if the table wants a science-fantasy look.

Damage

Damage Types Mostly Stay The Same

Use the normal fifth edition damage types. Most already translate cleanly into Stellar Dawn fiction.

Stable

Common Energy and Matter

Lightning can be electrical discharge. Thunder can be sonic shock or pressure waves. Fire, cold, acid, poison, piercing, slashing, and bludgeoning usually need no rule change.

Force

Force Stays Force

Force damage may appear as kinetic projection, gravitic impact, hard-light pressure, or pure arcane force. It keeps its normal damage type.

Later

Radiant and Necrotic

Radiant and necrotic damage may receive campaign-specific flavor after The Quiet Ones establishes what magic, metaphysics, and death mean in Stellar Dawn.

Economy

Use Standard Prices

Stellar Dawn keeps standard fifth edition prices and exchange rates for bookkeeping until a specific economy rule replaces them.

Bookkeeping

Coins Are Accounting Units

Copper, silver, gold, and platinum values may represent credits, station scrip, trade chits, account transfers, salvage value, faction currency, or other local money in the fiction.

Future Rule

Names Can Change Later

The campaign may rename the currency units, but the exchange should continue to match the standard fifth edition progression unless the DM announces a different economy rule.
